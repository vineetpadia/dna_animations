# Replication: teaching and scientific scope

The scene follows one fork. It first establishes antiparallel templates,
then opening, an RNA primer and leading-strand extension, repeated lagging
priming/Okazaki synthesis, primer removal and DNA replacement, ligation,
and semiconservative daughter duplexes. Bases and fragments are deliberately
shortened so a student can follow the direction and identity of the strands.

During the demonstration, fork motion is indicated but the fork pauses while
each mechanism is isolated for explanation. Real enzymes coordinate the
process in parallel, and the lagging strand can form a replication loop.
The cartoon does not show replisome architecture, clamps, topoisomerases,
proofreading, single-strand binding proteins, chromatin, origin licensing,
or species-specific primer-processing enzymes. Primer-removal machinery is
therefore named by its function. A one-tile RNA primer stands for a short
RNA segment. Daughter products at the end are a conceptual overview, not
the literal crop of the preceding incomplete fork.

The maturation view isolates an internal primer between neighboring fragments.
The right-hand fragment's 3′ end fills the gap toward the left; ligase then
joins this new 3′ end to the retained fragment's 5′ end on the left. This crop
does not imply that an isolated terminal primer can be replaced without a
neighboring extendable end.

Sources:

- [Hamdan et al., Dynamics of DNA replication loops reveal temporal control
  of lagging-strand synthesis (Nature, 2009)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07512)
  supports coupling, opposing extension directions and repeated priming.
- [Pandey et al., Coordinating DNA replication by means of priming loop and
  differential synthesis rate (Nature, 2009)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08611)
  supports the distinction between isolated teaching beats and coordinated
  leading/lagging synthesis observed in T7 replication.
- [Alberts et al., DNA Replication Mechanisms, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26850/)
  provides the textbook sequence of primer processing and nick ligation.

# Consensus shuffling

Based on [Binkowski et al., 2005, Nucleic Acids Research](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gni053).

The animation follows the paper's order: rehybridize a synthetic population,
fragment it, retain mismatch-containing fragments on immobilized MutS, and
reassemble unbound fragments through overlapping extension. PCR can then
amplify the products; the filtering/reassembly cycle can be repeated.

The example uses eight-base molecules and two-base overlaps so letters remain
legible; these are schematic regions, not working substrate lengths. Three
copies illustrate strand reassortment. The view then follows four fragments
from the pool. Two shorter ends leave the zoomed view rather than being
classified as biochemical waste. Ordinary DNA shuffling diversifies parental
combinations; this additional mismatch filter enriches the population consensus.

MutS recognizes and captures; it does not rewrite a base. Capture is drawn
clearly for two mismatches, without claiming perfect specificity or recovery.
The method reduces errors; it does not guarantee an error-free molecule or
recover an intended sequence absent from the input. Capture biases and PCR
errors are omitted. The final caption explicitly retains residual uncertainty.
